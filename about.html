2. Calculation Methodology & Mathematical Formulas

DSCR Analytics implements standard banking and commercial real estate underwriting standards. Below is an authoritative breakdown of how our engine computes each metric:

A. Net Operating Income (NOI)

Net Operating Income reflects the total operational cash generated by an income-producing property before deducting debt service (mortgage principal and interest) and income taxes:

NOI = Gross Annual Income − Annual Operating Expenses
  • Gross Income: Total rental revenue, laundry/parking fees, and ancillary income.
  • Operating Expenses: Property taxes, insurance, property management fees, maintenance, utilities, and vacancy reserves. (Capital expenditures and debt service are strictly excluded from OpEx.)

B. Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

The DSCR measures a property's cash flow cushion relative to its scheduled mortgage obligations:

DSCR = Net Operating Income (NOI) / Total Debt Service (TDS)

Interpretation: A DSCR of 1.00x means the property produces exactly enough revenue to cover its loan payments with zero cushion. A DSCR of 1.25x means the asset generates 25% more net cash flow than required to service the mortgage.

C. Periodic Debt Service (Fully Amortizing vs. Interest-Only)

Debt service represents the scheduled cash outflow required to satisfy loan obligations over a designated period:

  • Fully Amortizing Mortgage: Monthly debt service (M) is computed using the standard fixed-rate amortization equation:
    M = P × [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n − 1 ]
    where P is loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12), and n is total months in the amortization term.
  • Interest-Only (I/O) Option: When toggled, periodic debt service reflects interest charges alone:
    M = P × (Annual Interest Rate / 12)
    Underwriting Note: Most lenders require borrowers to qualify at the fully amortized payment rate even if an initial interest-only period is granted. Our tool displays the I/O DSCR to assist with initial cash flow analysis.

D. Maximum Qualifying Loan Amount

When underwriting a commercial loan, lenders solve backward from the property's NOI and their minimum required DSCR threshold (typically 1.25x) to calculate the maximum loan amount the property can support:

Max Loan = NOI / (Target DSCR × Annual Debt Constant)

The Annual Debt Constant (k) is the ratio of annual debt service to total loan principal: k = Annual Debt Service / Principal. By dividing the allowable debt service by k, we arrive at the exact capital limit supported by the property.

E. Lender Underwriting Benchmark Tiers

Commercial underwriters and non-QM loan programs categorize assets into four core risk profiles:

  • < 1.00x (Deficit / Under Water): The property operates at a negative cash flow after debt service. Traditional commercial lenders will reject this loan unless significant additional collateral, cross-default structures, or personal debt coverage are provided.
  • 1.00x – 1.24x (Marginal / Tight): Property breaks even or possesses a minimal safety margin. Lenders typically mandate lower Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratios (e.g., 60-65%), higher interest rate spreads, or substantial interest reserves.
  • 1.25x – 1.49x (Standard / Prime Benchmark): The golden standard for conventional commercial mortgages, Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac multifamily loans, SBA 504 / 7(a) debt, and standard non-QM DSCR programs. Provides a healthy 25% cash buffer.
  • ≥ 1.50x (Strong / Low Risk): Superior debt-coverage safety cushion. Borrowers often secure optimal pricing tiers, interest-only options, and maximum permissible leverage.
